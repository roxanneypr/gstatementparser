GCash protects your transaction history PDF with a password so only you can open it. Here's how to
open it, and what to do when it doesn't work.

## Why the PDF has a password

Your statement shows every transaction, the people and merchants you paid, and your balance. The
password keeps that private if the file is forwarded or your email is accessed by someone else.

## How to enter it

- **In this converter:** choose the PDF on the [converter page](/), type the password in the
  "PDF password" box and press Convert. The password is only used on your device to open the file.
  It is never sent anywhere or saved.
- **In a PDF viewer:** open the file and type the password when asked.

## If it says "Wrong password"

- Check uppercase and lowercase letters. Passwords are case-sensitive.
- Make sure there are no extra spaces before or after the password.

## Can the password be removed?

This converter can't remove, recover or guess a PDF password, and you should be careful with any
website that offers to. Many of those upload your file to their servers. Once your statement is
open, you don't need to remove the password: convert it to Excel and work with the spreadsheet instead.

## Keep your statement safe

- Don't post the password next to the file (for example, in the same chat message).
- Delete copies you no longer need from shared folders.
- Use tools that work on your device. This converter never uploads your PDF.
