Searching "PDF to Excel" gives you dozens of free converters. For a menu or a school form, any of
them will do. A GCash or bank statement is different, so it's worth a minute to check where your
file is going.

## What your statement reveals

A transaction history usually shows:

- your name and account or mobile number
- every person and shop you paid, and who paid you
- amounts, dates and times
- your balance over the whole period

Together that's a detailed picture of your life: where you shop, who your family and clients are,
and how much you have. It's also exactly what a scammer needs to sound convincing.

## Two kinds of online converters

**Converters that upload your file.** Your PDF is sent to the company's server, converted there,
and the result is sent back. Many of these are legitimate and delete files after a while. But your
statement still leaves your device, and you're trusting that company's security, its staff and its
promise to delete it.

**Converters that work in your browser.** The page downloads its code to your phone or computer,
and your PDF is opened and converted right there. The file never travels anywhere. Once the page
has loaded, the converter could even work without internet.

Both can look identical. The difference is where the conversion happens.

## How to check for yourself

You don't have to take anyone's word for it.

**Read the privacy policy.** Look for words like "upload", "our servers", "stored for" or "deleted
after X hours". If files are deleted after a time, they were uploaded first.

**Watch the Network tab (on a computer).** In Chrome or Edge, press F12 and open the **Network**
tab, then convert a file. An uploading converter sends a request about the size of your PDF. A
browser-based one only loads its own script files.

**Try it offline.** Load the converter, then turn off Wi-Fi and mobile data (or turn on airplane
mode) and convert your file. If it still works, nothing was uploaded. Some converters load their
tools only when you start, so run one test conversion first, then go offline.

## Other habits that keep you safe

- **Don't send your statement or its password to strangers.** Be careful with Facebook pages or
  Messenger accounts that offer to "convert it for you".
- **Don't post screenshots** of your transactions in group chats or public posts, even with the
  balance covered.
- **Use the original PDF.** A converter never needs your GCash MPIN, a one-time PIN (OTP) or your
  login. If one asks, close it.
- **Store the spreadsheet carefully.** The converted file holds the same information as the PDF.
